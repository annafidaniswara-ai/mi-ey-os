**Owner:** Product Owner

**Data policy:** Use fictional examples only. Do not store real or identifiable child data in this public repository.

## 1. Purpose and limits

This document proposes the structure for a practical framework that helps instructors plan movement opportunities, notice observable actions, adapt activities, and reflect on instruction.

It is **not** a diagnostic tool, clinical assessment, universal milestone chart, or pass/fail standard. Age bands are contextual prompts, not fixed expectations. Children may use different strategies, need different supports, or show different patterns across settings and days. One observation must not be used to infer a stable trait or diagnose a developmental condition.

This is a structure draft, not a complete competency inventory. The sample IDs and indicators below are illustrative design examples and have not been validated.

## 2. Framework design principles

1. **Observe before interpreting:** record what the participant did and the conditions; separate this from any interpretation.
2. **Flexible progression:** describe possible forms of exploration and coordination, not a required linear sequence or deadline.
3. **Multiple ways to participate:** adaptations are part of the design, not exceptions or labels.
4. **Play and agency:** invite, demonstrate, and offer choices; do not pressure a child to perform.
5. **Context matters:** space, surface, equipment, familiarity, fatigue, confidence, and instruction can affect what is observed.
6. **Safety first:** review environment, equipment, spacing, supervision, and stop/modify conditions before each activity.
7. **Evidence integrity:** distinguish research evidence from pedagogical choices; cite sources for scientific claims and disclose limitations.
8. **Privacy by design:** repository examples must be fictional; no names, images, birth dates, health details, or identifiable observation records.
9. **Human review:** educators and the Product Owner must review the framework before operational use.

## 3. Proposed domain map

### Domain A — Locomotor movement (MI-DOM-LOC)

**Working definition:** Movement that transports the body through space, such as walking, running, stepping over or around objects, jumping, or changing direction.

**Proposed competency clusters:**
- A1. Explore movement from one place to another.
- A2. Explore changes in direction, pathway, and speed in response to play cues.
- A3. Explore stepping over, around, or onto low, stable surfaces where appropriate and safe.
- A4. Explore take-off and landing actions through optional, low-risk play when suitable to the setting.

**Illustrative observable cue:** “Moves between two visible markers using a chosen strategy; may pause, change route, or seek support.”

**Possible flexible progression language:** from exploring movement in an open space → to moving between familiar markers → to varying route or direction with a cue. These are possible opportunities, not stages every child must pass through.

### Domain B — Stability and body control (MI-DOM-STA)

**Working definition:** Actions that help a participant manage body position and orientation, including maintaining or changing a position, stopping, turning, bending, reaching, and balancing with appropriate support.

**Proposed competency clusters:**
- B1. Explore stable and changing body positions.
- B2. Explore stopping, starting, turning, and changing orientation.
- B3. Explore reaching, bending, or shifting weight within a comfortable range.
- B4. Explore balance challenges with suitable support and a safe environment.

**Illustrative observable cue:** “Attempts to pause or change body position after a cue; uses a support or another strategy if helpful.”

**Possible flexible progression language:** from exploring positions with broad support → to changing position on a familiar cue → to combining a change of position with a simple movement game. Do not use a single balance attempt as a judgment of ability or development.

### Domain C — Object manipulation (MI-DOM-OBJ)

**Working definition:** Actions that move, control, or interact with objects, such as rolling, pushing, carrying, placing, kicking, throwing, or receiving a soft object.

**Proposed competency clusters:**
- C1. Explore moving objects by pushing, rolling, carrying, or placing.
- C2. Explore sending an object toward a broad target.
- C3. Explore receiving, stopping, or responding to an object at a comfortable distance.
- C4. Explore using different object sizes, textures, weights, and movement pathways, with safe equipment.

**Illustrative observable cue:** “Rolls, pushes, or releases a soft ball toward a large target using a chosen strategy.”

**Possible flexible progression language:** from free exploration of an object → to sending it toward a broad target → to varying distance, direction, or partner interaction when comfortable. Accuracy or speed is not a measure of worth.

## 4. Integrative competencies (MI-DOM-INT)

Integrative competencies combine two or more domains in a meaningful play context. They supplement the three core domains; they do not replace them.

### INT-01 — Move through space while adjusting body position
- **Domains linked:** Locomotor + Stability.
- **Illustrative invitation:** Follow a short marker path, pause at a visual cue, then choose a route around a soft obstacle.
- **Observable cues:** Moves along a route; attempts to pause or change direction; chooses or accepts an adaptation.
- **Adaptations:** Widen the route, shorten the distance, remove the obstacle, slow or repeat cues, offer a supported or seated alternative.
- **Safety note:** Avoid unstable, elevated, sharp, or trip-prone obstacles.

### INT-02 — Move and send an object toward a target
- **Domains linked:** Locomotor + Object manipulation + optional Stability.
- **Illustrative invitation:** Walk or move to a marker and roll a soft ball toward a large target.
- **Observable cues:** Transitions between movement and object interaction; tries a chosen way of sending the object.
- **Adaptations:** Move the target closer, enlarge it, change ball properties, allow a stationary version, or model the action.
- **Safety note:** Maintain spacing; use soft equipment and avoid sending objects toward faces.

### INT-03 — Adjust movement in a shared play space
- **Domains linked:** Locomotor + Stability + optional Object manipulation.
- **Illustrative invitation:** Participants move between broad markers and respond to a simple stop/go cue while leaving space for others.
- **Observable cues:** Changes or pauses movement in some way; navigates around others with support or cues.
- **Adaptations:** Reduce group density, create separate lanes, use visual cues, or offer an individual route.
- **Safety note:** This is not a test of compliance or social competence; prioritize space, supervision, and choice.

## 6. Observation and progression language

Prefer phrases such as:
- “explores…”
- “attempts…”
- “chooses…”
- “repeats or varies…”
- “with a cue, demonstration, support, or adaptation…”
- “in this activity and context…”

Avoid:
- “must be able to by age X”
- “failed,” “abnormal,” “lazy,” or “behind” based on one activity
- diagnosis or clinical inference
- ranking children against one another
- assuming one visible action proves mastery in all settings

A useful observation note, if needed for instructor reflection, records **activity + context + visible action + support offered + next instructional option**. Do not attach it to an identifiable child in this public repository.

## 8. Evidence base and careful interpretation

1. **World Health Organization (2019).** *Guidelines on physical activity, sedentary behaviour and sleep for children under 5 years of age.* The guidance addresses physical activity, sedentary behaviour, and sleep across a 24-hour day. It is not a detailed competency framework and does not validate the sample indicators in this document. https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789241550536
2. **Logan, S. W., Robinson, L. E., Wilson, A. E., & Lucas, W. A. (2012).** Getting the fundamentals of movement: a meta-analysis of the effectiveness of motor skill interventions in children. *Child: Care, Health and Development, 38*(3), 305–315. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2214.2011.01307.x
3. **Veldman, S. L. C., Jones, R. A., & Okely, A. D. (2016).** Efficacy of gross motor skill interventions in young children: a systematic review. *BMJ Open Sport & Exercise Medicine, 2*, e000067. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjsem-2015-000067
4. **Morgan, P. J., Barnett, L. M., Cliff, D. P., et al. (2013).** Fundamental movement skill interventions in youth: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *Pediatrics, 132*(5), e1361–e1383. https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2013-1167
5. **Engel, A. C., Broderick, C. R., van Doorn, N., Hardy, L. L., & Parmenter, B. J. (2018).** Exploring the relationship between fundamental motor skill interventions and physical activity levels in children: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *Sports Medicine, 48*, 1845–1857. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-018-0923-3
6. **Interventions to Promote Fundamental Movement Skills in Childcare and Kindergarten: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis (2017).** *Sports Medicine, 47*, 2045–2068. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5603621/

**Evidence caveat:** Reviews support the relevance of offering movement-skill learning opportunities, but findings vary by age, intervention, measurement, and study quality. Evidence does not justify treating the proposed age bands, cue wording, progression examples, or integrative competencies as validated norms. Verify each reference and its exact claim during the research review before publication.
