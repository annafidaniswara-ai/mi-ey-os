# MI-EY OS — Fictional Instructor Walkthroughs, Ages 2–4

**Document ID:** MI-EY-FWK-WALK-24-001  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Draft — illustrative walkthroughs; not field-tested or approved  
**Audience:** Movement instructors / early-childhood movement educators  
**Data policy:** All names and scenarios are fictional. Do not add real or identifiable child data.

## 1. Purpose

These walkthroughs test whether the draft competency dictionary and review matrix are understandable and usable in planning. They are desk-based examples, not evidence of developmental validity, safety approval, or effectiveness. They do not score or diagnose a child.

Each scenario follows: context → instructor invitation → observable description → interpretation boundary → possible adaptation/next option → matrix checks.

## 2. Walkthrough A — LOC-02: Explore changing direction and pathway

### Fictional setup
An instructor prepares a clear, flat indoor space with two broad floor markers and a wide route between them. The activity is offered to a fictional participant called “Participant A.” No real child record is represented.

### Instructor invitation
“Would you like to go to either marker? You can choose your route.” The instructor demonstrates once and allows the participant to watch, join, change route, or opt out.

### Example observation (fictional)
“During a short marker-path activity, Participant A moved toward the nearer marker, paused, and chose a wider route after the instructor pointed to it. The instructor used a gesture and allowed additional time.”

### Do not conclude
- Do not infer a developmental delay or stable ability from this event.
- Do not treat choosing the nearer marker or pausing as failure.
- Do not infer listening, attention, compliance, or language ability from the route choice.

### Possible next teaching option
Offer the same route again, remove one marker, or let the participant choose between two routes. Repetition, changing the activity, or stopping are all valid options.

### Adaptation and safety check
- Keep the pathway flat, uncluttered, non-slip, and wide enough.
- Use visual/gesture cues as well as speech.
- Avoid time limits, racing, and other participants crossing the route.
- Stop or simplify if the area becomes crowded or the participant appears uncomfortable.

### Review-matrix prompts
- Q01/Q02: Is the movement intention clear and the visible action separated from interpretation?
- Q03/Q04: Are route options flexible and is opting out respected?
- Q05: Are the floor, spacing, cue, and stop conditions explicit?
- Q07: Are inference limits clear?

## 3. Walkthrough B — STA-03: Explore reaching and shifting weight

### Fictional setup
A lightweight, soft object is placed within easy reach on a stable surface at floor level. The activity can be completed while seated or in another comfortable position.

### Instructor invitation
“Here is an object you can explore. Would you like it closer or farther away?” The instructor does not ask the participant to climb, stretch beyond comfort, or balance without support.

### Example observation (fictional)
“Participant B reached toward the object, then asked for it to be moved closer. The instructor moved it within reach. Participant B then picked it up using a chosen strategy.”

### Do not conclude
- Requesting the object be moved closer is not failure.
- Do not infer weakness, limited independence, or developmental status from this single action.
- Do not treat range of reach or posture as a pass/fail measure.

### Possible next teaching option
Offer a choice between two objects placed within comfortable reach, or allow exploration from a seated position. There is no requirement to increase distance or difficulty.

### Adaptation and safety check
- Use stable surfaces and light, age-appropriate objects.
- Do not use unstable furniture or elevated placement that encourages climbing.
- Keep the seated or supported option available.
- Stop or adjust if the participant appears uncomfortable or the environment becomes unsafe.

### Review-matrix prompts
- Q02: Does the note describe the action rather than label the participant?
- Q04/Q05: Are support, accessible placement, and safety constraints explicit?
- Q07: Does the interpretation limit avoid conclusions about general ability?

## 4. Walkthrough C — OBJ-02 + INT-02: Send an object toward a broad target

### Fictional setup
A soft ball and a large floor-level target are placed in a clear lane. The activity is available as a stationary version or a version that involves moving to a marker first.

### Instructor invitation
“You can roll or push the ball toward the big target, or watch first.” The instructor models a gentle roll, then lets the participant choose.

### Example observation (fictional)
“Participant C pushed the soft ball; it stopped short of the target. The instructor moved the target closer and offered another turn. Participant C chose to roll the ball again.”

### Do not conclude
- A missed target is not failure and does not establish poor coordination.
- Do not score speed, distance, or accuracy.
- Do not infer attention, motivation, or social competence from participation or opting out.

### Possible next teaching option
Repeat at the same distance, bring the target closer, use a larger ball, or leave the activity unchanged. Increase difficulty only if useful and safe, never because a progression step is mandatory.

### Adaptation and safety check
- Use soft equipment and a broad target.
- Keep the lane clear; prevent cross-traffic.
- Never aim objects toward faces or a crowded area.
- Offer stationary, seated, supported, and observation-only options.

### Review-matrix prompts
- Q03/Q04: Are alternative participation modes genuinely available?
- Q05: Are equipment and collision risks addressed?
- Q06: Does the integrative entry include both movement and object interaction where intended?
- Q07: Does the text avoid scoring or diagnostic inference?

## 6. Instructor feedback form (use without child data)

For each walkthrough, an instructor may answer:
1. Which sentence was clear and useful for planning?
2. Which instruction or safety condition is ambiguous?
3. Is the observable description distinguishable from interpretation?
4. Is at least one meaningful adaptation available?
5. Is the linked domain actually present in the activity?
6. What wording should change before use?

Record only instructor feedback about the document and activity design. Do not record participant names, identifiable stories, photos, dates of birth, health information, or child-level observations in this public repository.

## 8. Evidence context

Wick et al. (2017) reviewed 30 trials involving 6,126 preschoolers aged 3.3–5.5 years. Although pooled results favored interventions for measured fundamental movement skills, the authors rated certainty in treatment estimates as very low and noted limitations in study quality and long-term follow-up. This supports cautious exploration of structured movement opportunities; it does not validate these fictional scenarios or the MI-EY competency model. https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-017-0723-1

WHO (2019) provides population-level guidance on physical activity, sedentary behaviour, and sleep for children under five. It is not a competency-specific assessment standard. https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789241550536
