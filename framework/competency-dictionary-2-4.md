# MI-EY OS — Competency Dictionary, Ages 2–4

**Document ID:** MI-EY-FWK-DICT-24-001  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Draft — not reviewed or approved  
**Audience:** Movement instructors and early-childhood movement educators  
**Platform scope:** Ages 2–6; this dictionary covers the first framework tranche for ages 2–4  
**Data rule:** Fictional examples only. Do not store real or identifiable child data in this public repository.

## 1. Purpose and interpretation

This dictionary expands the proposed framework into reviewable competency records. It supports instructors in planning varied movement opportunities, describing observable actions, selecting adaptations, and reflecting on the environment and instruction.

It is **not** a diagnostic instrument, a clinical or school-readiness assessment, a ranking system, or a universal milestone chart. The 2–4 age range is a planning context, not a claim that all children should demonstrate each example by a particular birthday. A single observation cannot establish a stable ability, delay, or diagnosis.

The competency IDs and examples below are proposed content identifiers. They are not validated measures. “Flexible progression” means optional opportunities an instructor might offer next—not a required sequence, score, or deadline.

## 2. Evidence-use note

Research reviews report that structured fundamental-movement-skill interventions can improve some measured skills in young children, but study populations, interventions, outcomes, and certainty vary. A systematic review of childcare/kindergarten interventions included children aged 3.3–5.5 years and rated certainty in treatment estimates as very low; findings should not be generalized as validated norms for every 2–4-year-old. WHO guidance for children under five addresses physical activity, sedentary behaviour, and sleep at a population-guidance level; it does not prescribe the competency IDs or indicators below.

References are listed in Section 8. The research supports the general rationale for offering varied movement opportunities; it does not validate MI-EY's proposed indicators or progressions. Exact claims must be checked against full texts during review.

## 3. Record structure and review rules

Each record contains: stable ID; domain; intention; observable examples; non-examples; possible flexible progression; adaptations; safety notes; interpretation limits; cross-domain links; and review criteria.

### Observation language

Prefer: “tries,” “explores,” “moves using a chosen strategy,” “with a cue or support,” and “in this activity/context.”

Avoid: “failed,” “abnormal,” “lazy,” “behind,” “mastered for age,” or diagnosis based on a single activity. Describe the action and context separately from any interpretation.

A fictional observation format may be: **activity + context + visible action + support/cue offered + possible next teaching option**. Do not enter child names, birth dates, photos, health details, or identifiable records in this public repository.

## 4. Domain A — Locomotor movement (MI-DOM-LOC)

### LOC-01 — Explore moving from one place to another

- **Working definition:** Moving the body through available space using a self-selected strategy.
- **Learning intention:** Offer opportunities to travel between broad, visible points in a safe space.
- **Observable examples:** Moves from one marker toward another; pauses, changes route, or asks for support; uses an alternate movement strategy.
- **Non-examples / do not infer:** Do not require a particular gait, speed, distance, or independence. Do not infer a delay from not taking a particular route.
- **Flexible progression opportunities:** Explore an open area → travel between familiar markers → choose between two routes or respond to a simple cue. These options may be reordered, repeated, or skipped.
- **Adaptations:** Shorten distance; widen route; remove visual clutter; use a clear demonstration or visual cue; allow support or an alternate movement mode.
- **Safety:** Clear trip hazards, provide stable flooring and adequate spacing, supervise actively.
- **Cross-domain links:** STA-01 (position change), INT-01 (route plus pause).
- **Interpretation limit:** The observation describes one action in one setting; it does not measure general mobility or developmental status.
- **Review criteria:** Is the cue observable and nonjudgmental? Are alternate movement strategies accepted? Is the environment specified? Does the entry avoid a required age deadline?

### LOC-02 — Explore changing direction and pathway

- **Working definition:** Varying the route or direction of movement in response to space, play, or a simple cue.
- **Learning intention:** Invite route choices around broad markers or through an uncluttered pathway.
- **Observable examples:** Turns toward a marker; chooses a route around a soft, low-profile marker; changes direction after a model or cue.
- **Non-examples / do not infer:** Do not equate immediate response to a verbal cue with competence; hearing, language, familiarity, attention, and cue design may affect response.
- **Flexible progression opportunities:** Choose a route freely → follow a familiar visual route → choose between two routes or respond to a clear stop/turn cue.
- **Adaptations:** Use floor arrows or pictures; reduce competing cues; remove obstacles; allow more time; demonstrate rather than rely only on speech.
- **Safety:** No sharp or unstable obstacles; route must be wide and non-slip; avoid crowding.
- **Cross-domain links:** STA-02 (stop/start/turn), INT-03 (shared space).
- **Interpretation limit:** Not a test of obedience, attention, or language comprehension.
- **Review criteria:** Are the cue and response observable? Are visual/nonverbal alternatives provided? Are safety risks of route design addressed?

### LOC-03 — Explore stepping over or around low obstacles

- **Working definition:** Adjusting movement to pass around or over a low, stable object where appropriate.
- **Learning intention:** Offer an optional pathway variation with a clear bypass route.
- **Observable examples:** Walks around a marker; steps across a flat line or very low stable object; chooses to go around or asks for help.
- **Non-examples / do not infer:** Choosing the bypass is not failure. Do not require jumping, height, or speed.
- **Flexible progression opportunities:** Walk around an object → step across a flat visual line → choose between going around or over a low, stable object.
- **Adaptations:** Use tape/flat markers; reduce object height; provide a bypass; model slowly; offer a supported or seated alternative.
- **Safety:** Avoid raised, rolling, sharp, or unstable obstacles; inspect surface and footwear; one participant per space when needed.
- **Cross-domain links:** STA-03 (weight shift/reach), INT-01.
- **Interpretation limit:** The result is influenced by surface, confidence, novelty, footwear, and instruction.
- **Review criteria:** Is bypass treated as valid? Is the obstacle genuinely low and stable? Is there an explicit stop/modify condition?

### LOC-04 — Explore a change in pace or stop/start pattern

- **Working definition:** Varying movement pace or pausing and restarting in a play context.
- **Learning intention:** Offer a low-pressure game with clear visual or auditory cues and room to choose a comfortable pace.
- **Observable examples:** Pauses after a cue, restarts, or varies pace; responds after a model or additional time.
- **Non-examples / do not infer:** Do not score reaction time or frame delayed response as noncompliance.
- **Flexible progression opportunities:** Explore own pace → pause/restart with a familiar cue → vary pace or combine a pause with a route choice.
- **Adaptations:** Use visual cue cards; slow cue timing; reduce group size; allow observation before joining; remove competitive timing.
- **Safety:** Avoid racing in confined spaces; ensure sufficient stopping distance and spacing.
- **Cross-domain links:** STA-02, INT-03.
- **Interpretation limit:** Not a measure of impulse control, listening ability, or fitness.
- **Review criteria:** Are there non-auditory cue options? Is competition/timing avoided? Are space and supervision considered?

## 5. Domain B — Stability and body control (MI-DOM-STA)

### STA-01 — Explore body positions and transitions

- **Working definition:** Moving between comfortable body positions, such as standing, squatting, sitting, kneeling, or reaching a surface, as suitable to the participant.
- **Learning intention:** Invite participants to choose among several safe positions during play.
- **Observable examples:** Changes position; uses a hand or stable support; selects a different position to interact with an object.
- **Non-examples / do not infer:** Do not prescribe one “correct” posture or require getting up/down without support.
- **Flexible progression opportunities:** Explore positions with available support → move between two chosen positions → combine a position change with a simple object or story cue.
- **Adaptations:** Offer stable support; provide seated options; adjust surface height; allow extra time; keep choices voluntary.
- **Safety:** Use stable surfaces and clear floor space; never pull or force a participant into a position.
- **Cross-domain links:** OBJ-01 (object interaction at different levels), INT-01.
- **Interpretation limit:** A chosen position reflects task and comfort as well as movement capability.
- **Review criteria:** Are several positions accepted? Are support and voluntary choice included? Does wording avoid normative posture judgments?

### STA-02 — Explore pausing, starting, and turning the body

- **Working definition:** Managing a pause, restart, or change in body orientation during movement.
- **Learning intention:** Offer stop/go or turn games with simple, predictable cues.
- **Observable examples:** Pauses or changes orientation in response to a visual/modelled cue; uses extra time or a support strategy.
- **Non-examples / do not infer:** Do not label missed cues as defiance or a deficit; do not require an immediate response.
- **Flexible progression opportunities:** Explore pauses freely → respond to a familiar single cue → combine a pause with a direction choice.
- **Adaptations:** Reduce cue complexity; use pictures or gestures; repeat the demonstration; create an individual route.
- **Safety:** Avoid sudden stopping near obstacles or other participants; keep pathways open.
- **Cross-domain links:** LOC-02, LOC-04, INT-03.
- **Interpretation limit:** Cue response depends on how it is presented, understood, and experienced in that moment.
- **Review criteria:** Are cue alternatives available? Is the task noncompetitive? Are instructions and environmental conditions described?

### STA-03 — Explore reaching and shifting weight

- **Working definition:** Reaching toward an object or shifting body weight within a comfortable range and safe support conditions.
- **Learning intention:** Place an object at an accessible location so participants can choose how to reach or retrieve it.
- **Observable examples:** Reaches toward an object; changes stance or position; uses support or asks for the object to be moved closer.
- **Non-examples / do not infer:** Do not require a particular range, symmetry, or unsupported balance.
- **Flexible progression opportunities:** Reach to a nearby object → choose among objects at different accessible positions → combine reaching with a voluntary change of position.
- **Adaptations:** Bring object closer; vary height within a safe accessible range; allow seated participation; use lighter/larger objects.
- **Safety:** No unstable furniture or elevated reaching; objects should not encourage climbing.
- **Cross-domain links:** OBJ-01, STA-01, INT-02.
- **Interpretation limit:** Reach strategy varies with object placement, body position, familiarity, and comfort.
- **Review criteria:** Is the object reachable without unsafe climbing? Are support and seated options stated? Is range not treated as a pass/fail score?

### STA-04 — Explore balance with appropriate support

- **Working definition:** Maintaining or adjusting body position during a chosen static or moving activity, with support as needed.
- **Learning intention:** Offer broad, low-risk opportunities to pause, shift position, or move along a flat pathway.
- **Observable examples:** Pauses in a chosen position; uses a support; changes foot or hand placement; chooses a simpler version.
- **Non-examples / do not infer:** One wobble or loss of balance does not establish a trait or diagnosis. Do not require narrow beams, heights, or eyes-closed challenges.
- **Flexible progression opportunities:** Explore stable positions on a flat surface → pause on a broad marker → add a gentle direction or reach variation if appropriate.
- **Adaptations:** Widen base/path; add stable hand support; shorten the task; keep both feet on the floor; allow a seated or supported alternative.
- **Safety:** Flat non-slip surface, close supervision, no elevated or unstable equipment, immediate modification if unsafe.
- **Cross-domain links:** LOC-03, STA-03, INT-01.
- **Interpretation limit:** Balance behaviour is task-specific and can vary with fatigue, surface, footwear, and confidence.
- **Review criteria:** Are hazards and supports explicit? Is the example free from height/risk escalation? Does it avoid interpreting one attempt as developmental status?

## 6. Domain C — Object manipulation (MI-DOM-OBJ)

### OBJ-01 — Explore moving and placing objects

- **Working definition:** Moving objects by pushing, rolling, carrying, placing, or releasing them.
- **Learning intention:** Provide safe, varied objects and invite free exploration.
- **Observable examples:** Pushes or rolls an object; carries or places it; uses a different strategy or requests assistance.
- **Non-examples / do not infer:** Do not require a specific hand, grip, or technique; do not rank by speed or neatness.
- **Flexible progression opportunities:** Explore one safe object → move it between broad locations → choose among objects or routes.
- **Adaptations:** Change size, texture, or weight; use larger objects; lower target or container; allow two hands or partner support.
- **Safety:** Use age-appropriate, non-toxic, non-choking-risk equipment; inspect for damage; supervise object use.
- **Cross-domain links:** STA-01, STA-03, INT-02.
- **Interpretation limit:** Object properties and familiarity affect the chosen strategy.
- **Review criteria:** Are equipment properties and safety checks documented? Are different strategies accepted? Is the environment suitable for the intended age group?

### OBJ-02 — Explore sending an object toward a broad target

- **Working definition:** Directing an object toward a large, accessible target by rolling, pushing, underhand tossing, or another safe strategy.
- **Learning intention:** Invite attempts to send a soft object toward a broad target without scoring or competition.
- **Observable examples:** Rolls or pushes a soft ball in the target's direction; changes distance or strategy; chooses a stationary version.
- **Non-examples / do not infer:** Do not require accuracy, distance, speed, or a particular throwing form. Do not compare children.
- **Flexible progression opportunities:** Send an object freely → aim toward a broad nearby target → vary target distance or direction if desired.
- **Adaptations:** Move target closer; enlarge it; use a larger/lighter ball; allow a stationary or seated version; demonstrate.
- **Safety:** Soft objects only; establish clear lanes; never aim toward faces or crowded areas.
- **Cross-domain links:** LOC-01 (move to target), STA-03, INT-02.
- **Interpretation limit:** A missed target may reflect distance, object, cue, or chance; it is not evidence of failure.
- **Review criteria:** Is the target broad and safe? Are accuracy and competition excluded? Are adaptations listed?

### OBJ-03 — Explore receiving or stopping a moving object

- **Working definition:** Responding to an object that rolls or moves slowly toward a participant, including stopping, redirecting, moving away, or choosing not to engage.
- **Learning intention:** Offer a predictable, low-speed object interaction with an option to observe.
- **Observable examples:** Reaches toward, blocks, stops, redirects, or moves away from a slowly rolling soft object.
- **Non-examples / do not infer:** Do not require catching a thrown ball; opting out is not failure.
- **Flexible progression opportunities:** Observe a slowly rolling object → stop or redirect it at a comfortable distance → choose a partner or target variation.
- **Adaptations:** Reduce speed and distance; use a larger contrasting ball; roll instead of throw; allow a partner to model.
- **Safety:** Roll rather than throw at participants; ensure enough space; stop if an object interaction becomes unpredictable.
- **Cross-domain links:** STA-03, LOC-02, INT-02.
- **Interpretation limit:** Visual tracking, anticipation, comfort, and task familiarity can affect participation; this entry is not a sensory or clinical assessment.
- **Review criteria:** Is speed controlled? Is opting out allowed? Are throwing-at-people and diagnostic inference explicitly excluded?

### OBJ-04 — Explore object interaction with a partner or shared target

- **Working definition:** Taking part in a simple shared object activity, such as rolling toward a partner or taking turns placing objects in a container.
- **Learning intention:** Offer an optional shared game with clear space and a predictable sequence.
- **Observable examples:** Sends an object toward a partner or shared target; watches or waits; chooses parallel play or an individual version.
- **Non-examples / do not infer:** Do not use participation as a score for social competence or compliance; parallel play or opting out may be appropriate.
- **Flexible progression opportunities:** Explore individually near others → take part in a shared target game → choose a simple back-and-forth exchange when comfortable.
- **Adaptations:** Use parallel lanes; shorten distance; add visual turn cues; provide duplicate equipment; let an adult model.
- **Safety:** Maintain spacing, use soft equipment, supervise turn transitions, and avoid forced contact.
- **Cross-domain links:** INT-03, LOC-01, STA-02.
- **Interpretation limit:** Participation depends on familiarity, group size, communication style, and preference.
- **Review criteria:** Is individual/parallel participation accepted? Are turn cues accessible? Does the wording avoid treating social participation as a movement milestone?

## 7. Integrative competencies (MI-DOM-INT)

### INT-01 — Travel through space and adjust body position

- **Domains linked:** LOC-01 / LOC-02 + STA-01 / STA-02.
- **Illustrative invitation:** Move between broad floor markers, pause at a visual cue, and choose a route around a flat marker.
- **Observable examples:** Travels using a chosen strategy; attempts a pause or direction change; selects an alternate route.
- **Non-examples:** Do not require completion without support or interpret missed cues as a deficit.
- **Flexible progression:** Free route exploration → route with one pause cue → choice of route plus cue, if suitable.
- **Adaptations:** Widen/shorten route, remove markers, use visual cues, reduce group density, allow supported or seated participation.
- **Safety:** Flat uncluttered space, no unstable obstacles, adequate spacing.
- **Review criteria:** Both linked domains are genuinely represented; an alternate route is valid; cue and environment are clear.

### INT-02 — Travel and send an object toward a target

- **Domains linked:** LOC-01 + OBJ-01 / OBJ-02 + optional STA-03.
- **Illustrative invitation:** Move to a marker, then roll a soft ball toward a large target.
- **Observable examples:** Transitions between movement and object interaction; tries a chosen way to send the object.
- **Non-examples:** Do not score speed, accuracy, or sequence completion as a global competency.
- **Flexible progression:** Stationary object exploration → move to a nearby marker then send the object → choose distance or route variations.
- **Adaptations:** Keep the participant stationary; bring target closer; use a larger/lighter ball; allow a supported or seated option.
- **Safety:** Clear sending lane; soft equipment; never aim at faces; avoid simultaneous cross-traffic.
- **Review criteria:** Cross-domain links are meaningful, not merely listed; a stationary alternative exists; risk of collision is addressed.

### INT-03 — Adjust movement in a shared play space

- **Domains linked:** LOC-02 / LOC-04 + STA-02; optional OBJ-04.
- **Illustrative invitation:** Move between separate broad markers and respond to a simple stop/go cue while leaving space for others.
- **Observable examples:** Pauses, changes route, or uses a cue/support to navigate the space.
- **Non-examples:** Do not treat this as a compliance, impulse-control, or social-competence test.
- **Flexible progression:** Individual route → route with clear visual markers → optional shared route with reduced density.
- **Adaptations:** Separate lanes, reduce group size, use visual cues, allow individual play, slow the sequence.
- **Safety:** Active supervision, adequate spacing, unobstructed routes, clear stop condition.
- **Review criteria:** The activity remains safe for the group size; participation can be individual; the language does not infer personality or diagnosis.

## 9. Competency-level review rubric

For every competency, reviewers should mark each item **Pass / Revise / Not applicable**, with a short rationale:

1. **Construct clarity:** Is the movement intention defined in plain, nonclinical language?
2. **Observability:** Does the indicator describe visible action in context rather than an assumed trait?
3. **Non-normative progression:** Are progression options flexible, without deadlines, rankings, or universal age expectations?
4. **Adaptability and inclusion:** Are meaningful alternative routes, supports, and ways to participate provided?
5. **Safety:** Are equipment, surface, spacing, supervision, and stop/modify conditions adequate for the proposed activity?
6. **Cross-domain mapping:** For an integrative entry, are linked domains actually represented in the activity?
7. **Interpretation limits:** Does the record explain what cannot be inferred from the observation?
8. **Evidence traceability:** Are scientific claims tied to a reliable source, and are limitations stated?
9. **Privacy:** Are all examples fictional and free of identifying details?
10. **Instructor usability:** Can an instructor understand and use the record without specialist jargon?
11. **Version and review metadata:** Is status Draft/In review/Approved explicit, with reviewer and date only when an actual review occurs?

**Decision rule:** A record is not ready for operational use if any critical safety, privacy, interpretation, or evidence issue remains unresolved. “Pass” in this draft rubric is not a formal validation or clinical endorsement.
