# MI-EY OS — Content Model Validation Example

**Document ID:** MI-EY-VAL-001  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Draft — not approved  
**Purpose:** Demonstrate whether the proposed content model can connect competencies, movement experiences, and a lesson in a way that is usable by an instructor. This is a desk-check example, not a validated developmental assessment or approved activity plan.

## 1. Validation boundaries

- **Primary user:** instructor / movement educator.
- **Initial framework scope:** ages 2–4; the intended platform scope remains ages 2–6.
- **Domains in scope:** locomotor movement, stability, and object manipulation.
- **Data policy:** fictional examples only. Do not record real child names, dates of birth, photos, identifiable observations, or other personal data in this public repository.
- **What this validates:** whether the proposed fields and links are understandable and traceable.
- **What this does not validate:** developmental norms, learning outcomes, effectiveness, medical suitability, or operational safety in a particular venue.

## 2. Fictional scenario

An instructor is preparing a short, play-based movement session for a mixed-readiness group in an early-learning setting. The instructor needs to see the intention, setup, adaptations, safety checks, and observable cues without diagnosing or ranking individual children.

No individual child record is created. The scenario is generic and fictional.

## 3. Example competency records

### MI-COMP-0001 — Move through space

- **Domain:** Locomotor movement.
- **Definition:** Explore moving from one place to another using a self-selected walking or running pattern in a clear, supervised area.
- **Age context:** Illustrative planning context for ages 2–4; not a universal milestone or pass/fail criterion.
- **Observable indicators:** Moves between two visible markers; changes direction when the path is clear; pauses or changes pace when prompted.
- **Non-examples:** Do not label a child “behind” because they choose to walk, pause, or observe.
- **Adaptations:** Shorten the route; widen the path; allow walking instead of running; offer a visual demonstration.
- **Evidence note:** This is a program-design example. It is not presented as a validated measure.
- **Status:** Draft.

### MI-COMP-0002 — Maintain and change body position

- **Domain:** Stability.
- **Definition:** Explore maintaining or changing a body position during simple play, with freedom to use support or choose a comfortable variation.
- **Age context:** Illustrative planning context for ages 2–4; individual readiness and context vary.
- **Observable indicators:** Pauses in a chosen position; bends and returns upright; steps around an obstacle at a self-selected pace.
- **Non-examples:** Do not infer a disorder or stable ability from a single attempt.
- **Adaptations:** Use a wider, lower obstacle; allow hand support; remove the obstacle and practise stopping/starting.
- **Evidence note:** This is a proposed instructional description, not a diagnostic criterion.
- **Status:** Draft.

### MI-COMP-0003 — Explore object control

- **Domain:** Object manipulation.
- **Definition:** Explore sending, receiving, rolling, or carrying a soft object in a safe, open space.
- **Age context:** Illustrative planning context for ages 2–4; the object, distance, and task should be adapted.
- **Observable indicators:** Rolls a soft ball toward a broad target; carries and places an object; responds to an invitation to retrieve an object.
- **Non-examples:** Do not require a specific throwing or catching form as a universal age standard.
- **Adaptations:** Use a larger, lighter, softer object; shorten distance; use a stationary target; allow rolling rather than throwing.
- **Evidence note:** This is a proposed instructional description, not a validated measure.
- **Status:** Draft.

## 4. Example movement experiences

### MI-MOVE-0001 — Follow the open path

- **Competency links:** MI-COMP-0001.
- **Intention:** Explore moving between markers and changing direction.
- **Setup:** Two or three flat, high-contrast floor markers on a non-slip, unobstructed surface.
- **Steps:** Invite participants to move between markers at their chosen pace; demonstrate walking; offer optional changes of direction.
- **Adaptations:** Shorter distance, wider path, walking-only option, or observing before joining.
- **Safety checks:** Keep the path clear; use flat markers; prevent crossing traffic; ensure active supervision; stop if the surface or spacing becomes unsafe.
- **Observation cues:** Participant chooses a route, moves between markers, pauses, or changes direction.
- **Status:** Draft; venue-specific review required before use.

### MI-MOVE-0002 — Pause, bend, and reach

- **Competency links:** MI-COMP-0002.
- **Intention:** Explore changing body position during a simple play sequence.
- **Setup:** Open space with no hard or sharp obstacles.
- **Steps:** Invite participants to pause, reach toward a visible marker, and return to a comfortable position. Participation can be seated, standing, or supported.
- **Adaptations:** Place the marker higher or lower; reduce repetitions; allow hand support; remove any time pressure.
- **Safety checks:** No forced positions, balancing challenges at height, or physical handling without appropriate consent and training.
- **Observation cues:** Participant tries a position change, uses support, pauses, or chooses an alternative.
- **Status:** Draft; venue-specific review required before use.

### MI-MOVE-0003 — Roll to the wide target

- **Competency links:** MI-COMP-0003.
- **Intention:** Explore sending an object toward a broad target.
- **Setup:** Soft, lightweight balls and a large target on the floor, with a clear rolling lane.
- **Steps:** Demonstrate rolling; invite participants to roll from a self-selected distance; retrieve objects together when the lane is clear.
- **Adaptations:** Larger or lighter ball; closer target; wider target; seated or standing option.
- **Safety checks:** Use soft objects; separate lanes; do not aim at people; retrieve only when the area is clear.
- **Observation cues:** Participant sends, pushes, rolls, carries, or retrieves an object.
- **Status:** Draft; venue-specific review required before use.

## 5. Example lesson record

### MI-LESSON-0001 — Explore, pause, and roll

- **Learning intention:** Offer opportunities to explore locomotor movement, body-position changes, and object control through simple play.
- **Competency links:** MI-COMP-0001, MI-COMP-0002, MI-COMP-0003.
- **Experience links:** MI-MOVE-0001, MI-MOVE-0002, MI-MOVE-0003.
- **Illustrative duration:** 10–15 minutes, adjustable to the group and context; not a prescribed physiological dose.
- **Sequence:**
  1. Welcome and demonstrate available choices.
  2. Follow the open path.
  3. Pause, bend, and reach.
  4. Roll to the wide target.
  5. Close with a calm transition and optional verbal reflection.
- **Instructor role:** Demonstrate, supervise, offer choices, observe without ranking, and adapt the environment.
- **Observation plan:** Note only general, non-identifying patterns about activity setup and participation; do not create a named child record in this repository.
- **Stop/modify conditions:** Unsafe surface or crowding, equipment failure, distress, fatigue, or any condition where safe supervision cannot be maintained.
- **Status:** Draft; not an approved lesson plan.

## 7. Evidence boundary

The WHO 2019 guidelines address physical activity, sedentary behaviour, and sleep for children under 5, and emphasize varied physical activity and active play. They do not validate the specific competency IDs, activities, age-band labels, or instructional cues in this example.

Reference:
- World Health Organization. (2019). *Guidelines on physical activity, sedentary behaviour and sleep for children under 5 years of age.* https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789241550536

Any later evidence review should evaluate sources for the specific claims being made and record limitations. Do not use a broad activity guideline as proof that a particular exercise is effective or safe in every setting.

## 8. Validation decision

**Current result:** The proposed schema passes a limited structural desk-check: the records can be linked and include basic instructor, adaptation, safety, and observation fields.

**Unresolved:** Instructor usability, field necessity, terminology, age/readiness interpretation, and venue-specific safety have not been validated by practitioners.

**Next action:** Ask the Product Owner and qualified movement educators to review this example. Keep all records in Draft until that review is explicitly completed and recorded.
